He poured cooking oil into the sea, with a pirate ship so close behind them he could hear the pirates laughing. His captain grabs him by the collar and goes, "We're about to get boarded, and you're making soup?" But he doesn't even look up. He just points at the black clouds. "Steer into the storm." The captain yells that they'll never survive it, and all he gets back is, "Trust the oil." But then a grappling hook clangs off the railing. So the captain turns the wheel and the pirates couldn't be happier. Why waste a cannonball when the storm will do it for free? Deeper in, the waves get taller than the mast, and the crew is tying themselves to anything bolted down, but he's still pouring, a little at a time, always on the side the wind is coming from. Then the pirates swing in to board, and up ahead a wave big enough to swallow both ships rises out of the dark. You see, sailors really carried oil just for this. They called it storm oil.
