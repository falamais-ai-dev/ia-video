He scanned a customer's lottery ticket, saw it had won a million dollars, and said, "Sorry, man, not a winner." The guy just shrugs, says, "Story of my life," and walks out. The second the door closes, the cashier is elbow-deep in the trash can, fishing the ticket back out and tucking it into his wallet like it's been his all along. By midnight, he's already bought a boat, a mansion, and a whole new set of friends, at least in his head. So, the next morning, he drives 2 hours to the lottery office, slides the ticket under the glass, and the lady at the counter smiles and says, "Wow, congratulations! Just wait right here." So, he waits. 5 minutes go by, then 10, until finally a door opens behind him. In walks the guy from the store, except now he's wearing a suit with a badge on his belt, followed by two police officers. You see, in real life, lotteries noticed that store clerks were winning way more often than everyone else, which is statistically a little suspicious. So, they started printing winning tickets as bait and sending undercover agents into stores pretending to be regular customers.
